# Harness API and mechanics

Reference for `window.__GAME__` (`src/game/harness.ts`) and the rules behind it. The short version and the conventions are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md); how to play and check the UI is in [AGENT_PLAY.md](AGENT_PLAY.md).

The economy is four phases (tables in `src/data/phases.ts`, gear in `src/data/gear.ts`, capital in `src/data/capital.ts`). Every purchase price (skill XP, job XP, gear, capital) is multiplied by `PURCHASE_COST_SCALE = 0.85` in `src/data/costs.ts`; the exported rows are already scaled, so read costs from the data modules, never from memory. Payouts and gates are not scaled. Gates latch: owning what a gate leads to (a gig, a job, a capital row, any gear) keeps it open.

- **A Skill grind.** 104 skills, bought with XP only. The used laptop's first copy (skill 1, `firstXpCost`) costs 90 XP so the first unlock comes within a minute; later copies and the skills after it cost a lot of clicks, and the next one stays locked until lifetime XP climbs past the last purchase. There is no $/s.
- **B Gigs.** Gig Board opens at 14,000 lifetime XP with skills 15 (Portfolio) and 16 (React) owned (`GIG_BOARD_UNLOCK`). 65 gigs, each cleared once for a lump $ and an optional `retainerCps`. Each gig requires every earlier gig plus a skill set that grows by one skill each time (gig 1 needs skills 1–6), capped at the skill table. Gig 1 also needs 12,000 lifetime XP. Gig 8 ("Site maintenance retainer", 2 $/s) is the first retainer; gigs 1-7 pay a lump only.
- **C Jobs.** Job Board opens at 40,000 lifetime XP and 6 cleared gigs (`JOB_BOARD_UNLOCK`). 71 job rows in a tree (each held once, salary $/s; twelve of them, the learning, teaching and research roles, also add idle XP/s through `xpPerSec` in `src/data/phases.ts`, and eleven pay a one-time lump of XP on hire through `xpLump`, added to both `xp` and `xpTotal`). Taking a job costs spendable XP (`xpCost`: a quarter of its `xpReq`, then the purchase scale; the Internship is free), never money. Job 1 (Internship offer) needs 35,000 lifetime XP; job 14 (Senior SWE) needs 160,000 XP.
  **Specializations** (`src/data/specializations.ts`, rules in `src/game/build.ts`). The ladder is a tree: ranks 1-7 are a shared trunk (ids 1-7), ranks 8-15 are one track per specialization, ranks 16-31 are shared again (ids 16-31). Six specializations: `frontend` (click), `backend` (balanced), `infra` (idle), `ai` (idle), `fde` (click, tag product), `security` (balanced). A track has eight rows with the same `xpReq`, price, pay, XP and click numbers as the backend row at its rank, so every track paces alike. Backend keeps ids 8-15; the others are `100 * k + rank` (frontend 108-115, infra 208-215, ai 308-315, fde 408-415, security 508-515). A track row wears the art of the backend row at its rank.
  The **pick** opens once a job of rank 7 is held and is made once. **Every job above rank 7, the rows 16-31 included, needs a picked specialization**, and a track row also needs that track; other tracks' rows are never shown and `buyJob` refuses them, so capital cannot be reached through the ladder without a pick (holding $500,000 still opens it). The **switch** is once a career: it keeps money, gear and the trunk jobs (and shared rows already held), drops the old track's job ids, and the player starts at the new track's first row. Gates latch: `build.peakRank`, the rank held before the switch, keeps capital open and gear revealed (`heldRank` in `build.ts`). `nextUnlock` names `{ key: "spec:pick", name: "Pick a specialization", tab: "C", needs: the six names, either: true }` when the pick is the next step.
  **Leadership** (`src/data/leadership.ts`, rules in `src/game/build.ts`). After Senior SWE the ladder forks once more: `ic` (Individual contributor) or `manager` (Manager). The **pick** opens once a job of rank 14 is held, is made once and is locked (no switch). **Every job above rank 15 needs a picked line**, and rows 16-24 also need their own line: IC is ids 16, 17, 19, 23, 24 (Staff engineer, Staff platform, Principal, Distinguished, Fellow); Manager is ids 18, 20, 21, 22 (Engineering manager, Director of Eng, VP Engineering, CTO); rows 25-31 are shared, and rank 15 stays in the specialization tracks. The other line's rows are never shown and `buyJob` refuses them (`jobState` reads `hidden`, or `lead` before the pick). An IC career sees 27 rows, a Manager 26. Ids and ranks did not change. **Hiring belongs to the Manager**: `hire` is refused unless the line is `manager`, and each kind opens at a manager rank (`outsourced`, `bootcamp` at 18; `copypaste`, `senior` at 20; `staff`, `tenx` at 21; `greybeard`, `codegod` at 22), on top of the row-above-has-a-hire chain. Hires already held (an old save) keep growing the product and drawing pay under IC; they just cannot be bought. **The win's ending follows the line** when one is picked (`ic` night owl, `manager` sponsor); with none, the hires' share decides as before. Picking never closes anything (gates latch) and a specialization switch keeps the line. The pick is a journal `pick` line with `ref: "lead:manager"`. `nextUnlock` names `{ key: "lead:pick", name: "Pick a leadership line", tab: "C", needs: the two names, either: true }` once the track is held to rank 15.
  **Click and idle builds** (numbers in `src/game/build.ts`). A click build's *manual* click multiplies the jobs' click share by `CLICK_BUILD_CLICK_MULT` (2.5 over the plain ladder's 0.68), so with every job its build can see held, 2 clicks a second pay 5 times the idle $/s. Hover clicks and auto-click never get that multiple (`click("auto")` in the store); an idle build's penalty still applies to them. An idle build earns `IDLE_BUILD_IDLE_MULT` (1.25) times its idle $/s on every source, offline credit included, and half the click share. Balanced builds are the plain ladder. `getState().clickShare` and `clickPower` report the manual click.
- **Your company** (the globe, the fleet, the world and a status page, `src/components/globe`). The globe is a small one on the desk, bottom left under the career log (`MiniGlobe`, shown once Your company is unlocked, hidden below 860 px, captioned with the users; its ring turns peach when serving is slow or dropping some requests and red on an incident or when most requests drop, a red badge counts the live incidents, and `GlobeFeed` says one line at a time above it what just happened: an incident that started or ended, a region's first users, a milestone, requests dropping or recovering (`feed.ts` is the pure part; nothing shows while the away note is up; below 860 px the activity-bar globe button carries a red dot while an incident is live)); drag turns it. There is no top bar: the users of 8B are in the mini globe's caption and the Company tab. The activity-bar globe button switches the shop to its **Company** tab (shown if the shop is hidden; `src/components/globe/CompanyTab.tsx`, a sixth shop tab keyed `W` in `ShopTab`, open with the studio, and never part of `nextUnlock` or the phase takeover), one narrow column of the status banner and live incident, the metrics, the world (led by one next-step line from `src/components/globe/nextStep.ts`: no users, a data law, dropped requests, incidents, near capacity, then the cheapest region to buy), the world (users against the 8B, resistance, regions, and a line for the Team: its size and payroll, with the Hire and Fire buttons in the Jobs tab), points of presence, cities, fleet, footprint, uptime bars and past incidents. The goal is `WORLD_TARGET` = 8,000,000,000 users (`src/game/globe/world.ts`). Users are tracked per region (`company.users`, regions in `src/data/regions.ts`, people per region in `world.ts`; the app can reach `REACH_CEILING` = 99% of them, about 8.2 billion in all). Each cleared gig seeds its client's region with `SEED_PER_GIG` users (gig ids walk `CLIENT_CITIES`). A region grows by `GROWTH_PER_SEC × reach × max(users, seed) × (1 − users/ceiling) × served × (1 − resistance/200) × the growth multiplier` (`productFactsOf`: 1, scaled by the perks and Prestige, plus what the hires add); `reach` is 1 for a bought region and 0.15 for one not bought, times how many points of presence it has live (three are full coverage) and how near the nearest one is, so locations drive users. A small share of all users spills into every region. Users send `RPS_PER_USER` = 0.1 requests a second each, so demand is `demandOf(users)` (a DDoS surge multiplies one region's share); capacity is `capacityOf(equipment)` (a base laptop, `BASE_RPS` = 10 requests a second or 100 users, plus the serving gear kinds). The share over capacity is dropped: the dropped users churn away and the drops cost `REVENUE_PER_DROPPED_RPS` dollars a second, so gear is the throttle and every new region forces a gear purchase. **Users pay.** Served users pay `arpuOf(users)` dollars a second each (`load.ts`: $0.0002 at scale, up to 81 times that for the first few thousand, so 1k users pay about the internship and 1M about the Senior SWE; table in `docs/PACING.md`), and dropped users pay nothing. `servingOf(...).revenuePerSec` is that total, `stepCompany` and `advanceCompany` return it as `earned` next to `lost`, and `settleGrowth` adds `earned` before charging `lost`, so the tick and the return from away both pay it (the away note's credit includes it). The status bar's `$/s` (`StatusRates`) and the Company tab's "Revenue" figure include it; `getState().cps` does not. **The world fights back.** A global resistance meter (0 to 100, `resistanceTarget` of the users, with a lag) drags growth by up to half and makes incidents more frequent, harder and longer. Incidents are regional and capped at three live (`rollIncident`, `world.ts`): a DDoS surge multiplies a region's traffic, a censorship block stalls it and bleeds its users (a server there halves the bleed), a data-residency law does the same to a region without a live server (and never rolls where one is live), and a rival app halves its growth. They start once there are `INCIDENT_MIN_USERS` users, end on their own, or end at once when the player pays minutes of what the company earns (`incidentCost`; Respond in the world pane), which also takes 3 points off the resistance. **The win.** Reaching 8 billion latches `company.won`, writes the `world:won` journal line, shows a card in the world pane and multiplies everything the company earns by `WIN_MULT` (×1.25, or ×1.5 when won as a founder) for good; the game keeps going. Milestones at one million, one hundred million and one billion are journaled too, as are incidents. Growth, incidents and the win are stepped by `stepCompany`/`advanceCompany` (`src/game/globe/growth.ts`, in steps of `STEP_MS`, so offline time plays out too); the world rules are in `world.ts`. The status page keeps one bar per status day (`STATUS_DAY_MS`, 90 kept) in `company.uptime`, and the page is derived from those numbers (`src/game/globe/status.ts`).
- **Gear** (its own shop tab, between Jobs and Capital; table in `src/data/gear.ts`). Opens at 10,000 lifetime XP. 110 repeatable items bought with `buyEquipment`. Later rows also wait on a job id. Only the Cloud, GPUs, Racks, Orbit and Mars sections serve requests (`SERVING_KINDS`, `servedUsersOf` in `load.ts`): each copy serves `cps × 12` requests a second, so `cps × 120` users. Those section headers carry a "Serves users" tag and their rows say how many users a copy serves; Desk, Home lab and Storage add no capacity.
- **10x dev skills** (`gold` in the code and the harness: `src/data/gold.ts`, rules in `src/game/gold.ts`). Seven one-off perks bought once with spendable XP, shown in their own "10x dev" sub-tab of the Skills tab with a trophy and a description. A row opens at its lifetime-XP milestone and waits on the earlier row of its family. `buy`, `buySkill`, `buyEquipment`, `hire` and `fire` always act on one copy. **Hover clicking** (2, 5, 10, 15 clicks/s from 20,000 XP, and the first tier, Hover Tap, also waits for a Senior SWE job: a row can carry `unlockJobRank`; the endgame 20/s still comes from owning every catalog row) makes `hoverRate` positive. **Time away** raises the offline cap from 8 to 12, 16 and 24 hours. Prices are a quarter of the opening XP, then the purchase scale. Saves keep them in an optional `gold` list.
- **D Capital.** 25 rows, from an angel syndicate seat to Mars Holdings. Opens on a Senior job (rank >= 14, held now or before the switch) OR $500,000 held at some point (`peakMoney`, so spending it later does not close the gate). Eras by id: mid 1-5, upper 6-11, q3 12-14, end 15-25. Only `buy(1..25)` is valid. Row 1 costs $85,000 and row 3 costs $1,700,000 after the scale.

- **Growth and hires** (`src/game/globe/product.ts`, `hires.ts`, `growth.ts`). Every career is the same: jobs pay salary and teach XP, and the product grows at the player's own pace (`GROWTH_PER_SEC`, a multiplier of 1) plus what the hires add (`growthMult` in `ProductFacts`), all scaled once by the Prestige bonus and Viral loop. Hires (`hire`, `fire`; the Team rows of the Company tab) cost a salary a second each, which `settleGrowth` takes out of the balance with the drops (it stops at 0). The win at 8B pays ×1.25 (`WIN_MULT`), once, and picks the ending from `hiredShare`. The fork (quit the job, burn, runway, bridges, the founder ending) was removed; `docs/COMPANY_ARC.md` has the history. The hires are in the save as an optional `hires` block (`docs/SAVE.md`).
- **Demand scale.** `demandOf(users)` is `users × 0.1`: a thousand users send 100 requests a second, a million 100,000, and eight billion 800 million. `src/game/globe/world.test.ts` pins the calibration: one copy of every serving row up to $1M serves over a hundred thousand users but not a billion, and five copies of every serving row serve all eight billion. Points of presence can be switched off one by one or by region (`footprint.off`, `footprint.dark`, both optional in the save); traffic goes to the nearest one still on.
- **The kitchen** (table in `src/data/combos.ts`, rules in `src/game/combos.ts`, wired in the store's career subscriber; every catalog row carries tags, `src/data/tags.ts`). Owning two ingredients that go together (a catalog row, or a tag held enough times) reveals a combo: a name, one clean joke and a small effect. Each effect is 1 to 5 percent on idle $/s, on click ($ and XP) or on idle XP/s, added together (not compounded) and capped at 25% per kind (`COMBO_CAP_PCT`). A combo is found the moment both ingredients are held, from any purchase or force helper, journaled once as a `combo` entry in the career log and saved as the optional `combos` id list. The bonus is read from what is held (`comboFactors`, folded in by `modsOf`), so `cps`, `clickPower`, `xpPerClick`, `xpPerSec` and the offline credit already include it. An Exit and `reset()` clear it. There is no kitchen panel yet; read `getState().combos`.
- **Prestige: the Exit** (`docs/PRESTIGE.md`; data in `src/data/legacy.ts`, rules in `src/game/legacy.ts` and `src/game/perks.ts`; the buttons are in admin mode only). Every career builds one product; `exit()` sells it. It pays `floor(cbrt(earnings across all careers / PRESTIGE_EARN_SCALE))` Prestige in total, so an Exit pays the rungs this career climbed (earnings are dollars credited, `stats.lifetimeEarned`, spending ignored; `setMoney` raising a balance counts), plus `ENDING_BONUS` (5) the first time a career wins the world with an ending (`nightOwl`, `sponsor`), which does not count as an earnings rung. The Exit is open on a later career from the start and on the first once it would pay at least 1 (a win alone does), and it needs a total of at least 1. It starts over money, XP, skills, gigs, jobs, gear and capital, the click boost and event multipliers, and the whole product: users, regions bought, hires, resistance, incidents, the win and its multiplier. A new product name is drawn and the start card reopens on the product beat (the handle stays). Prestige, `prestigeTotal`, Legacy perks, the Hall of Fame, 10x dev skills, the career log and the timeline stay. `prestigeTotal` gives `1 + 0.03 × prestigeTotal` on every income and XP stream (through `modFactors`, so the offline credit gets it) and on product growth. Legacy perks (three tracks, each after the one before, 95 Prestige in all) apply now and at the start of every career: Network (1, 2, 4, 7, 12) is a head start, Founder (2, 11, 16) is 1,000 users at the home region, growth ×1.5, product revenue ×1.5, and Capital (2, 4, 7, 11, 16) is offline cap +4 h, capital rows −10%, gear rows −10%, salaries and retainers ×2, capital income ×1.5. The Sell button is in the Company tab and in the Prestige panel (the crown on the activity bar), with a confirmation card (the first Exit asks twice) and a headline card.
- `cps` is idle $/s from owned items (retainers + salaries + equipment + capital). Click boosts do not change it. Product revenue from users is separate, in `world.revenuePerSec`; the status bar's $/s shows both.
- `clickPower` is the base $ payout. Every click always pays `xpPerClick × activeBoost.mult` XP; dollars wait until the first job. After that, clicks also pay `clickPower × activeBoost.mult`. Idle $/s still starts at the first retainer.
- Money mode starts at the first cleared gig (or whenever money already exists, as with a forced balance).
- Skills cost XP and spending never lowers `xpTotal`. Skills and jobs cost XP; equipment and capital cost money. Repeatable items cost `floor(base × 1.15 ^ owned)`.
- `nextUnlock` is the one thing to chase across the whole game. It names the first skill that has not been bought yet, not a closed tab. Once every skill is owned, closed gates come first: Gear (10K XP), Gig Board, Job Board, then Capital. With every gate open it walks Gigs, Jobs, Gear, then Capital and stops at the first row that has not been bought yet. An earlier unlocked row that was never bought wins over a later row. The first copy is enough for repeatable gear, skills and capital. `needs` is never empty. With Senior's track held to rank 15 and no line picked, the Jobs panel's step is `lead:pick` ("Pick a leadership line"; `needs` lists the two names, `either` is true). With Mid-level SWE held and nothing picked, the Jobs panel's step is `spec:pick` ("Pick a specialization"; `needs` lists the six names, `either` is true), after the gigs and before gear and capital in the whole-game walk.
- The shop has no per-tab banner. A closed board shows its lock panel (what it still needs); the rest is the row's own lock. `getState().nextUnlock` is still the whole-game suggestion (`nextUnlock(progress)` in `src/game/nextUnlock.ts`).
- `spriteTierId` is the art id of the stage sprite: the highest owned capital tier, else the art of the furthest job, gig or skill. It is `1` before the first purchase. `era` is `early` until a capital tier is owned.
- The shop lists owned rows plus the next unowned row, even while that row is still locked. Skills cannot be bought until `xpTotal` reaches `unlockAtXp`. Gear and capital first copies wait until their reveal gate (held money, and a job for some gear). `buy(tierId)` still enforces the capital gate.
- `tick(ms)` advances idle time immediately, capped at 8 hours. It can also roll the normal boost clock. Skills keep studying (XP/s) in every phase, and held jobs that teach add to it.
- `forceBoost(mult, ms)` replaces the current click boost. Use this instead of waiting for a random roll. On screen a boost only turns the balance card (`HeroMoney`) the theme accent, gold at ×10 and up, with the multiplier and a time bar at its foot (`data-testid="hero-boost"`); there is no boost chip on the stage. Event multipliers (`foreverMult`, and each live `tempMults` entry with its seconds left) are lines under the balance on the same card (`HeroMods`, `data-testid="forever-mult"` and `temp-<target>`).
- `advanceOffline(ms)` writes `savedAt` that far in the past and rehydrates. Credit is idle × seconds, capped at 8 hours (`offlineCapHours`, longer with Time away gold skills), for both $ and XP. The toast is a sticky note on the desk: one diary sentence, a newline, then the credit (`credited $X`, `studied +X XP`, or both). The sentence follows the career (studying, a retainer, a job, senior, empire) and rotates once per hour away. The credit math is unchanged.
- `forceEvent(id)` opens a random event now (ids 1–128, `src/data/events.ts`) without applying it. Returns false for an unknown id. An open event is replaced, never stacked. `acceptEvent()` takes the deal and returns false when a trade's cost cannot be paid. `dismissEvent()` declines and changes nothing. `event` is the modal on screen, `lastEvent` is kept after either choice. Stakes are minutes of what you currently earn (idle, or half a click per second when that is higher), and at least 1% of the balance you hold. A gift only adds money or XP. A trade swaps one for the other. Nothing only deducts. Random events roll roughly every 240s from rows whose `eras` include `any` or the current era. Optional `requires` (job, gig, gear, specialization, leadership line) is AND across fields and OR inside a list; a row with no `requires` stays era-only. Ids 111–128 are a build's own (each specialization and both lines have some), and they only roll for a career that has picked it. Cash events (money gifts and every trade) wait until money exists. A loss never pushes money or spendable XP below 0, and lifetime XP never falls.
- `forceReview(promptId?)` opens a 1:1 for the highest job you hold (`src/data/reviews.ts`). It returns false with no job or when `promptId` is outside that job's band. Omit `promptId` to roll one. It replaces an open event or review without applying it. `answerReview("exceeds")` is a money gift (12 minutes). `answerReview("meets")` closes the meeting and changes nothing. `answerReview("pip")` trades money for XP (10 minutes) and returns false when that cost cannot be paid, leaving the meeting up. `dismissReview()` leaves without a reply. `review.replies[].lines` are the same `+$` / `-XP` strings as events; meets has none and shows no change. A 1:1 rolls about every 12 minutes, only once a job is held, and waits out an event or a meeting already on screen. If both clocks are due, the event wins and the 1:1 slips 5 seconds. A 1:1 that names a specialization or line (ids 14–21) is only in the pool of a career on it.
- `foreverMult` (permanent) and `tempMults` (timed, one per target) from events are already folded into `cps`, `clickPower`, `xpPerClick` and `xpPerSec`, so the formulas above still hold.
- Completing a gig pays its lump with a confetti burst on the stage and nothing else: no card, no centered pop, nothing to dismiss.
- The window bar across the top (`TitleBar`, `data-testid="titlebar"`) is for show: red, yellow and green lights that do nothing, and the game's name. The balance card shows the balance, the XP and the click rates; the status bar shows the idle rates (`StatusRates`: `per-sec`, `xp-per-sec`); every rate is at the top of the metrics modal (`useLiveRates`).
- Below 860 px the desk's status bar is not shown, so the idle rates are only in the metrics modal there. The emoji.gg credit is the last line of the settings menu at every width. Its controls (Sound, Click, Rosters, Theme) are in the settings menu (`EditorSettings.tsx`), and the settings gear carries a green mark while a new setting is unpicked. Picking a setting clears its mark.
- Theme changes cross-fade (`ideTheme.ts`, View Transitions, 220 ms; instant without the API or with reduced motion), and a hover preview only paints once the pointer rests on a row for `PREVIEW_REST_MS` (120 ms).
- The UI font is a preference like the theme (`src/game/uiFont.ts`, rows in `src/data/uiFonts.ts`; `setFont(id)` here). It paints `--font-display` and `--font-scale` on the root; the `--fs-*` type tokens carry the scale, so each face is sized to about the footprint of Chakra Petch. Chakra Petch is in the main CSS; the other four load their latin 500, 600 and 700 files on the first hover, focus or pick (or when the settings menu opens, so each row can show its own face). Metrics stay on the system mono stack in every face. `MOBILE_CHECK_FONT=all npm run check:mobile` plays every face.
- The desk behind the stage is an activity bar (the Your company globe, the career log, GitHug, LinkedOut, Stats, the repo's star (not on a phone) and the settings gear at its foot; on the bare desk, before the bar shows, the gear is in the stage's top-right corner), the career log sidebar and a status bar. The status bar names the band the career is in (High school to Mars, by stage art id, `src/data/idePacks.ts`). `previewEra(era)` shows that era's first band there with a PREVIEW chip, the way the shop's Capital era tab does; `previewEra(null)` or 20s without interaction (`tick`) snaps back, as does buying into a new era. The snapshot has no field for it; read `data-preview` on `[data-testid='ide']`.
- One tab plays at a time (`src/game/openGame.ts`): the first holds the `tech-ascension-writer` Web Lock. A second tab shows "open in another tab" with Play here, which asks the playing tab over a BroadcastChannel to save once more and stop writing (`handOffCareer`), then boots with that save; the old tab reloads into the same screen. A tab that does not answer in 4 s has its lock stolen. A waiting tab also starts by itself when the playing tab closes. `__GAME__` exists only in the tab that plays.

- A save from any other `version` is wiped on load. There is no migration.
- `lastPop` is the last Balatro money pop: `medium` on a paying click, `large` on a gig clear and a phase unlock.

- **Career timeline** (table in `src/data/features.ts`, gates in `src/game/features.ts`, journal in `src/game/careerLog.ts`, wiring in `src/game/careerSync.ts`). The desk is earned: a new career has XP and the Skills tab. the first job (the internship) opens Your company (the activity-bar globe button; its reveal card's Try it opens the shop's Company tab; the Gear tab then lists the next serving row, Cloud and below, beside the next desk row, so the first dropped requests have gear to buy), Git + GitHug hygiene (5) the career log and the GitHug profile (a contribution graph from the journal, pinned repos, and the real repo's Star pill, which stays off screen until then), Portfolio site live (15) LinkedOut, the first 10x dev row in reach the 10x dev tab, 10K XP the Gear tab, the first dollar the $ counters (`$/click` shows once a job pays it), the first gig the rosters, the first job the metrics, the Thocky keyboard click sounds, the Drawing tablet editor themes, a hover skill hover click, the headset music, the first capital row of the mid era the gif parade (with all of its paths and categories), and the first job the desk cat. - **Identity** (`src/game/identity.ts`, start card in `src/components/IdentityPicker.tsx`). Every new career (a fresh save or `reset()`) draws a Tailscale-style handle (`quiet-otter`) and a product name (`Otterly`: the handle's animal plus one of eight suffixes), and opens a three-step start card with Next and Back: who you are (handle, Reroll), what you are building (product, its own Reroll that changes only the suffix) and the bet (Start keeps both for good; `keepProfile()` clears the card from any step). Rerolling the handle redraws the product. The product names the globe in the UI (the activity-bar button, the Company tab, the status page, the mini globe). There is no real name; GitHug and LinkedOut (`linkedout.com/in/<handle>`) both go by the handle. Nothing is ever typed. It is saved in an optional `profile` block with `product` and `chosen`; a save is only accepted with a handle the lists can draw, and a product the handle's animal cannot draw is replaced by a fresh one. A save from before handles gets one, already kept, with no card; one from before products gets a product, keeping `chosen`.
